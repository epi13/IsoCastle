# Design

## Player promise

IsoCastle is a readable, deterministic dungeon journey with the tactile specificity of early graphical RPGs and the usability of a modern desktop game. Every step is a decision, but the world breathes between decisions through snow, ember, water, light, and idle animation.

## Pillars

1. Legible tactics: the preview shows action cost, accuracy, damage range, resistance, and affected tiles.
2. Curious exploration: noise, light, search, secret routes, locks, hazards, and seeded floors reward attention.
3. Expressive builds: equipment and skills combine with eight Practices rather than locking players into classes.
4. Consequence without cruelty: choices reshape later conversations and the ending; critical progress is never trapped behind one roll.
5. Earned atmosphere: original art and procedural audio contrast hearth warmth with moonlit stone and the uncanny Bell Below.

## Turn model

Each action spends 100 energy by default. Speed determines how quickly an actor returns to 100 available energy. Movement, search, attacks, items, and spells all use the same scheduler. Diagonals are legal only when neither orthogonal corner is blocked. Presentation speed never changes outcomes.

## Progression

The player selects a background, presentation, portrait, two attribute emphases, one trained skill, a starting kit, and a first Practice. Levels grant an attribute point on alternating levels and skill points every level. The Hearth-Reader in Greywake offers a costly respec after Act I.

## Difficulty

Story, Journey, and Grim settings modify enemy planning depth, resource generosity, and recovery rules. They do not hide formulas or simply multiply all enemy health.

## Campaign scale

Three acts span Greywake, the Bracken March, the layered Underbell vaults, the icebound Lorn Shelf, and the airborne Rime-Crown. A normal playthrough targets 10–14 hours; exploration and alternate faction outcomes add replay value.
